Every crop ripens its seed in one of two places: in dry pods, husks and heads, or inside juicy fruit. Find which kind yours is, check whether it is time to pick, then follow the one short method for that kind. The guide ends when the seed is dry enough to put away.

<QuickAnswer>
If the seed sits in dry pods or heads (beans, peas, corn, grains, lettuce, the cabbage family), let the plants dry, break them up, then sieve and winnow. Tomato and cucumber seed stays in its own juice in a jar until the jelly around each seed falls apart, which takes about three days for tomatoes in Lofthouse's routine; then add water and keep what sinks. Melon, watermelon and squash seed skips the jar: just rinse it. Whatever the crop, finish by drying the seed fast, spread out thin.
</QuickAnswer>

## When to pick

Find the line that matches your garden this year.

<Points>

- **Frost is on its way.** Before the first hard frost, bring in every fruit you want seed from, ripe or not. Seed that freezes while it is still wet inside the fruit can be badly hurt. In Lofthouse's experience, fully dried seed of temperate crops is not harmed by freezing.
- **Your season is short.** Pick the fruit you have. In Lofthouse's experience, seed can grow long before its fruit is fully ripe, though the plants may come up less vigorous, and it keeps maturing for a while in a picked fruit.
- **Pods split before the plant has dried.** That is <Term id="shattering">shattering</Term>. Take the plants now and finish drying them on a tarp under cover, where rain and dew can't reach them.
- **Weeks of rain are forecast.** A passing shower does dry seed on the plant no harm, but a long wet spell brings mould. Move the plants somewhere dry and airy, and thresh later.
- **None of these.** Leave pods and heads on the plant until they crackle, and let fruit ripen fully.

</Points>

## Dry seed

<Steps>
  <Step title="Dry">Start only once the material is brittle and crackling. Damp pods smear instead of breaking.</Step>
  <Step title="Thresh">Break the pods or heads so the seed falls free. This is <Term id="thresh">threshing</Term>.</Step>
  <Step title="Screen">Shake the seed through a sieve or a piece of mesh that holds back stems and large bits: <Term id="screen">screening</Term>.</Step>
  <Step title="Winnow">Pour the seed slowly in a breeze or in front of a fan so the light <Term id="chaff">chaff</Term> blows away: <Term id="winnow">winnowing</Term>.</Step>
</Steps>

Use only as much force as the pods need. Lofthouse's own way is to knock bunches of dry plants on the inside wall of a bin until nothing more falls out.

The sieve and the breeze also take out many weed seeds, since they differ from crop seed in size or weight. Each one you remove now is one you won't sow next spring.

Grains go through the same steps on a larger scale. Sunflower heads left to dry whole can mould in a damp autumn, as Lofthouse found; seed rubbed out of the head and spread thin dries far faster.

## Wet seed

Wet seed usually comes out while you prepare the fruit for a meal, so keep a labelled jar or a colander next to the chopping board.

<Note>The jar is seed, not food. Label it, move it out of the kitchen, and make sure no one mistakes it for a drink or a sauce.</Note>

The table above tells you whether your crop needs the jar. Tomato and cucumber seeds wear a <Term id="gel-coat">gel coat</Term>, a jelly that stops them sprouting inside the fruit, and the jar lets it rot away: <Term id="seed-fermentation">seed fermentation</Term>. Melon, watermelon and squash seed has little or none to lose.

## Both routes: dry it fast and thin

Whatever the route, seed that stays damp moulds. Spread each batch in one layer somewhere airy, stir it now and then, and let it dry right through. Wet seed then gets one more winnowing: empty seed coats are light and blow off, and the good seed stays. Put the seed away once it passes the dry check in [Storing seed](/guides/storing-seed/#is-it-dry-enough-to-seal).

## How clean is clean enough?

Home-saved seed doesn't need to look like a shop packet. In Lofthouse's view seed is tough, and a little chaff sown along with it does no harm. Aim for seed that is dry, free of mould and mostly free of weed seed.
