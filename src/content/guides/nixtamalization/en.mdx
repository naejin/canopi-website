Why is corn for tortillas cooked with lime? Can the flour corn you grew become hominy or masa? Is pickling lime dangerous? This guide explains what nixtamalization is, why cooks do it and which corns suit it. It is not a recipe: the method uses caustic materials, so take the how-to from your national food-safety authority.

## What it is

Nixtamalization means cooking dry, ripe corn kernels in an alkaline liquid, water made basic with lime or ash. The base breaks down the tough outer skin of each kernel, the pericarp, which loosens or dissolves and is then washed away.

Two bases are common:

- **Pickling lime**, sold for food use. It is what Lofthouse uses.
- **Wood ash**, the older, traditional choice.

Both are caustic. That is the whole danger of this guide, and the section [The caustic danger](#the-caustic-danger) comes back to it.

## Why cooks do it

**Dough.** Ground nixtamalized corn holds together as a dough you can press and shape, for tortillas or tamales. Corn that is simply milled will not do this: mixed with water, it gives a sticky paste that falls apart. Lofthouse puts this down to the alkaline bath changing the corn's proteins.

**Nutrition, in Lofthouse's view.** He calls the process critical for two reasons: it lowers the toxins that moulds leave in grain, and it frees the niacin in corn, a vitamin that protects against pellagra, a deficiency disease. Read "lowers" as lowering, not removing. {/* general tip, not from the book */} Mouldy corn is not food, whatever you do to it.

**Taste.** Lofthouse loves the flavour so much that he only buys tortillas or corn chips listing lime among the ingredients.

## Which corns

Here is what Lofthouse says of each type of corn he grows, in the kitchen.

| Corn | Kernel | In the kitchen, per Lofthouse |
|---|---|---|
| Flour | Soft, easy to grind | His flour corn makes tender hominy; local cooks make tortillas, posole and hominy from it |
| Mixed grain corn | Flour, flint, dent, sweet and pop in one population | He uses it as hominy |
| Flint | Hard, dense, glassy | Hard on kitchen equipment; gritty as flour |
| Sweet | Wrinkled when dry | He eats it fresh, or parched in a hot pan with a little oil |

Flour corn is also known as a food-security crop. Lofthouse selected his own flour corn <Term id="landrace">landrace</Term> out of a mixed grain corn. To grow and select it, see [Corn](/guides/corn/).

- **Hominy:** the whole treated kernels, plump and soft.
- **Posole:** a dish, often a stew, made with hominy. The word is also used for the kernels themselves.
- **Masa:** the dough made by grinding the treated kernels, for tortillas and tamales.
- **Masa harina:** the dry flour. Lofthouse makes his own by drying the treated corn and then grinding it.

## The caustic danger

Pickling lime and wood ash are strong alkalis. They burn skin and eyes, and they are harmful if swallowed. The danger lasts through the whole process: the powder, the hot bath and the liquid you pour away all carry it.

<Safety title="Lime and ash are caustic">They burn eyes and skin and are harmful if swallowed: a splash or puff of powder can injure the eye. Protect your eyes and hands, and keep children and pets out of the kitchen while you work.</Safety>

- **Storage.** Keep the powder in a labelled container, out of children's reach.
- **Food grade only.** Use only lime sold for food. Building and garden lime are not the same product. {/* general tip, not from the book */}
- **Ash varies.** The strength of ash changes from one fire to the next, so you cannot know how caustic a batch is. {/* general tip, not from the book */}

## Lofthouse's practice, in words

Every cook seems to have a favourite recipe, Lofthouse notes, and his is only one of them. This is his practice, described so you know what the process involves. It gives no amounts on purpose: follow your food-safety authority.

<Steps compact>
  <Step title="The bath">He covers dry corn with water and adds pickling lime.</Step>
  <Step title="The heating">He heats it until the skins come loose. How long that takes depends on the corn and on the base.</Step>
  <Step title="The rinse">He tips the corn into a colander and washes off the skins and the lime.</Step>
  <Step title="Hominy or masa harina">He cooks it on as hominy, or dries and grinds it for masa harina.</Step>
</Steps>

Some recipes leave the corn standing in its liquid, before or after heating it. Lofthouse finds it makes no difference he can notice.
